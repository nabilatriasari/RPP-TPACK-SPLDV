Real-World Context:

At SMA Nusantara's Eco-Festival, the Student Council operates a zero-waste refreshment booth. They sell two popular organic items: Bamboo Cold Drinks (x) and Banana-Leaf Pastries (y). Two study groups purchased packages, but the individual item price tags were misplaced!

Group A bought: 3 Drinks + 2 Pastries for Rp 38,000
Group B bought: 2 Drinks + 4 Pastries for Rp 44,000

Branching Decision: Choose Your Strategy Route

Select how you want to analyze and tackle this system:

Route Alpha

Elimination Strategy

Equalize the coefficients of either Drinks (x) or Pastries (y) to eliminate one variable instantly.

Route Beta

Substitution Strategy

Isolate one variable (e.g. from Group B's equation) and substitute its expression into Group A's equation.

Route Gamma

"What If" Price Shock

What if sugar supply costs increase the drink price by Rp 2,000? How does the total spending budget adjust?

Question 1 (HOT - Mathematical Modeling):
A local artisan cooperative produces handwoven caps (x) and embroidered scarfs (y). A cap requires 2 hours of loom work and 1 hour of embroidery. A scarf requires 3 hours of loom work and 2 hours of embroidery. The total available loom time is 48 hours, and total embroidery time is 28 hours. Which system of linear equations correctly represents this constraint?
A) 2x + 3y = 48  and  x + 2y = 28
B) 2x + y = 48  and  3x + 2y = 28
C) 5x + 3y = 76  and  x + y = 28
D) 3x + 2y = 48  and  2x + y = 28
Question 2 (Conceptual - System Consistency):
Consider the system: 2x + 4y = 12 and x + 2y = 10. What is the geometric and algebraic interpretation of this system?
A) The lines intersect at (2, 2); unique solution.
B) The lines are identical (coincident); infinitely many solutions.
C) The lines are parallel with equal slopes but different intercepts; no solution (inconsistent).
D) The system has negative coordinates only.
Question 3 (Real-World Interpretation):
By solving a school ticket sales system, you obtain (x = 35, y = -5) where x represents adult tickets and y represents student tickets sold. How should an astute mathematician evaluate this result?
A) Mathematically valid and realistically plausible: student tickets were discounted.
B) Mathematically solvable, but in the real world physical ticket counts must satisfy x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0, indicating an error in model constraints or recorded revenue data.
C) The solution means 5 student tickets were refunded and cancelled.
D) Convert -5 to +5 and proceed.
